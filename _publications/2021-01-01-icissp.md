Security analysts need a way to decide which local-language reports deserve investigation. This paper develops a Turkish-language prototype that filters posts and news, tracks unusual attention around named targets and raises possible incidents for review. Co-authored with [Prof. Cengiz Acartürk](https://acarturk.net/), my M.Sc. thesis advisor at Middle East Technical University, and published at ICISSP 2021.

## From text to an investigation queue

The method connects language processing to event detection through a sequence an analyst can inspect:

1. **Find relevant terms.** Starting without an annotated Turkish security corpus, compare reports from before, during and after a known incident. TF-IDF helps identify candidate keywords; tests check the useful reports and false alarms each term introduces.
2. **Normalise the language.** Process Turkish word forms through the ITU NLP service so suffixes and surface variations do not prevent matching.
3. **Track a target over time.** Count mentions of predefined organisations, institutions and countries in the filtered text. Compare each target with a threshold derived from its own history.
4. **Review the evidence.** An unusual count raises an alert. An analyst checks the underlying reports to decide whether they describe an incident.

## Where the workflow could help

**Local incident monitoring:** organise reports about a service or institution into a queue for investigation. **Threat-intelligence triage:** surface local-language news and user reports, including vulnerability warnings. **Research data collection:** gather candidate security documents for subsequent review and labelling.

These are potential uses of the workflow. The publication evaluates a research prototype using historical Turkish Twitter and newspaper data; it does not establish production performance in other settings.

<details>
<summary>Study scope and evaluation</summary>
<p>The evaluation included confirmed incidents and false alerts. It did not establish how many events were missed. A conversational message containing a security term can still trigger an alert without describing an incident, which is why source review remains part of the workflow.</p>
<p>Applying the approach to another source, language or time period requires fresh evaluation of the vocabulary, text processing and alert thresholds. The paper reports the full experimental counts and acceptance criteria.</p>
</details>
