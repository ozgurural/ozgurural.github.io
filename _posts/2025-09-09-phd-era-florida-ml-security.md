I completed my Ph.D. in Electrical Engineering and Computer Science at Embry-Riddle Aeronautical University in the United States in August 2025. My dissertation is [Enhancing Proof-of-Learning Security Against Spoofing Attacks Using Model Watermarking](/publication/2025-dissertation).

## The research question

Proof-of-Learning concerns evidence that a model was trained rather than simply obtained from someone else. My research examines spoofing attacks against that evidence and the use of model watermarking as an additional verification condition.

The [feature-based watermarking paper](/publication/2024-ieee-access-watermarking), [SecurePoL paper](/publication/2025-secureproofoflearning), and dissertation describe the methods and experiments. Their reported results apply to those experimental settings; they do not establish an aerospace deployment or certification result.

## Connecting research and engineering

My background in cybersecurity, mission-control software, and flight simulation shapes the questions I bring to research: what is being verified, what assumptions does verification depend on, and what evidence would expose a failure?

The research and engineering work are related interests, but a connection between fields is not itself a measured transfer result. Applying a research method to a different operational setting would require its own evaluation.
