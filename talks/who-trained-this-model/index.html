Motivation

A copy of a model carries no trace of who trained it.

An AI model learns from examples, and what it learns is stored as a file of numbers. Training is costly. Copying the file is free, and the copy is identical.

Question 1 · effort

Did they do the training?

A record of the training run could show it.

Question 2 · identity

Is it their model?

A hidden mark inside the model could show it.

Background · Proof-of-Learning

Training leaves a trail. Proof-of-Learning keeps it.

Training adjusts those numbers step by step so the model makes fewer mistakes: picture a walk downhill on a landscape of error.

The trainer keeps snapshots, with the data and settings behind each step. A verifier re-runs the biggest steps and checks where they land.

The premise: faking a record that replays should cost as much as the training itself. So a record that replays is evidence of the work.

Jia et al., Proof-of-Learning: Definitions and Practice, IEEE S&P 2021

Background · model watermarking

A model watermark is a secret only the owner can test.

For example
A cat, with a small pattern in the cornera question only the owner knows to ask
owner's model: "ship"any other model: "cat"

In the best-known kind, the model also learns the answers to a few secret questions while it trains. Ask them later: only the owner's model answers.

The idea is old: mapmakers hid a street that does not exist.

Its limit: a mark shows whose model it is. It says nothing about how the model was trained.

The problem · spoofing

The check accepts “close enough”,
and close enough can be forged.

No two computers repeat a long calculation bit for bit, so every re-run step is allowed a tolerance: the tube.

A forged trail, built backwards from a finished model, stays inside the tube at every step, and passes.

So the trail alone cannot settle a claim. It needs a signal the trail does not contain.

Zhang et al., IEEE S&P 2022; Fang et al., IEEE EuroS&P 2023

Method · SecurePoL

Learn the mark inside the recorded training.
Accept only if both checks pass.

The mark is learned in the same recorded run. A claim must pass both checks: the trail and the secret.

A forger's trail can pass, but the forged model cannot answer. Rejected.

The same, in symbols

Trainminimise Ltask + λ · Lmark

Acceptre-run error ≤ δ  and  mark score ≥ τ

λ: how strongly the mark is learned · δ, τ: the thresholds of the two checks

Method · three kinds of watermark, compared

Where the secret lives decides
how it is checked and what it costs

1 · Trigger images

Secret inputs, planted answers

The cat that answers "ship". Checked just by asking the model, so it works even when the model is only reachable online.

Costs the most training time
2 · Weight signature

A pattern in two weights

A signature is written into two of the model's numbers. Checked by reading those numbers directly.

Almost free in time; costs a little accuracy
3 · Side branch

A signature beside the task

A small extra branch learns the mark, beside the classifier's own task.

Almost no accuracy cost; moderate time

Three established designs, one from each family, compared under the same training. The contribution is joining them to Proof-of-Learning, not new watermarks.

Why the two checks protect each other

Each check covers the other's blind spot.

Erase the secret, and the model changes: it no longer matches its own trail.

And the other way round
????????

Fake the trail instead, and the secret remains. With trigger images, a forger who never saw the questions can only guess. One lucky answer is easy; twenty questions with ten possible answers each is a chance of one in 1020.

Results · the headline experiment

Forged snapshots fooled the trail check.
The secret check caught every one.

Check 1 alone: each forged snapshot sits just inside the tolerance, so Proof-of-Learning accepts the forgery.

Check 2, at the same moments: the forged model does not carry the owner's secret, so the claim is rejected.

Drawn after Figure 20 of the paper: a forgery attack on Proof-of-Learning ("blindfold Top-Q"), image classifier on CIFAR-10.

Limitations, as the paper states them

What the study does not show,
and the research it opens

One small benchmark. A compact image classifier, not a language model.
Test it on large models and on fine-tuning
The trail grows with the model. For a billion-parameter model, terabytes of snapshots.
Store only what changes; record selected layers
Known attacks only. It makes them expensive; it does not prove every attack fails.
Tie the secret into the trail itself, so neither can change alone
Cryptographic proofs are stronger, but far more expensive, and they do not show whose model it is.
Combine with zero-knowledge proofs and trusted hardware

What the study contributes
  1. Why Proof-of-Learning's tolerance can be exploited, and why a second, independent signal is needed
  2. SecurePoL: a secret planted in the recorded training, and a claim accepted only if both checks pass
  3. A controlled comparison of three watermark designs under the same training, with budgets set in advance and repeated runs
  4. Open code and configurations that reproduce the results: github.com/ozgurural/SecurePoL-with-Watermarking
